# Review record: the keepalive and server time specification

## The change

Class S. Issue #81 (plan id M2a-64). This is the first version, written
from documents only; the issue stays open for the version that adds the
observation lab's and the test cell's facts
(keepalive-and-server-time.md, section 13, Q1).

- `docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md`: the keepalive procedure, its
  connection, identity and caps; how callback state is established on
  the client; when keepalives are sent; the exact conditions under which
  a keepalive's reply raises callback trust, what a raise covers (the
  lowering count) and its end; failed and local outcomes; the K step
  that makes every read and change of trust one step over the server
  key's record; server time ("not measured" in M2a, and only able to
  shorten a promise once measured); the time fields; counters, status
  and tests.
- `docs/specs/callbacks.md`: the lowering count beside the fence tuple
  (not part of it), compared in the trusted branch of 5.6, point 5,
  raised at every lowering and at the end of a raised trust (including a
  network change); the K step; the failed and local outcome split in 5.6,
  point 11; test C25.
- `docs/specs/README.md`: the index row, register rows E75 (the AFS
  registry's file service procedure table), E76 (RFC 4787) and E77 (two
  OpenAFS reference pages), and this specification added to E11 and
  E12's "Cited by". E75 is the same page as the VL and FS read
  specification's row E5 and merges into it once both are on `main`.
- **Written by** `claude-code:claude-opus-5-5` (drafting, and the fixes of
  every round).
- **Merge order.** The specification rests on the callback specification
  (#66), the Rx specification (#55), ADR 0006 and the seams
  specification (#71), and on ADR 0004's amendment in #53, point 3; it
  merges after them and does not leave draft before #53 is accepted.

### Round 1: review of `b85db41..0c3b8d7`, fixed in `ac9a4d3`

#### GLM 1 (major)

Location: docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:794-801 (5.6, point 10; with 5.6, point 6, condition 6 and callbacks.md 5.6, point 11)

Defect (verbatim):
> The ordering claim "A lowering by any other cause that races the outcome is either before the check, so condition 6 fails, or after the raise, so it lowers the raised trust: no order lets a raise survive a lowering that was recorded before it" is asserted without any mechanism making it true. The seven checks and the raise are listed as sequential steps "on the housekeeping thread", while lowerings are recorded "in the core" from other threads: a failed data-call outcome (Timeout/Aborted) is handled by the call's worker (C39, point 3), and a lapse is applied by whatever thread serves an entry of K (callbacks.md 5.6, points 5 and 11). A lowering that is recorded between condition 6's read of `lowerings` and the raise's write of `trust` is in neither of the dichotomy's two cases: it does not fail condition 6 (already read) and it does not lower the raised trust (the raise overwrites it afterwards). Unlike callbacks.md's own atomicity stories (the suspect epoch's single atomic fetch-and-add in 5.6, point 1; the tick run "as one step on the callback thread" in 5.6, point 14), the spec never says the handling is one core operation or one critical section of K's record. Concrete failure: a hostile server (or an on-path attacker) answers a keepalive promptly (receipt R), then withholds a concurrent status call's answer so its Timeout lands inside the outcome-handling window; the raise then writes trust until R+E on evidence that predates the recorded loss, and entries validated after the lowering — whose recorded lowering count equals the current one — are immediately served under C32, point 3's uncapped branch for up to E (120 s), exactly the revival across a known loss of continuity that the whole 0c3b8d7 amendment exists to forbid (callbacks.md 5.6, point 7; C25). Fix: state that the lapse check, the seven conditions and the raise are one atomic core step over K's record (as callbacks.md does for its tick), and give `lowerings` the same atomic-take story `suspect_epoch` has.

Consequence (verbatim):
> A raise can survive a lowering recorded before it, so trust is re-established on a reply that predates a known loss of callback continuity and post-loss entries are served without revalidation for up to E; the amendment's central invariant (a keepalive never revives trust across a loss, C32.3 / ADR 0006 D1.5-D1.6) does not hold as specified, and point 10's [OWN] claim is false for a real interleaving.

Disposition: FIXED. Chose one atomic core step, not a compare-and-swap: keepalive 5.6, point 10 ("The K step") makes every lowering, every end, every raise, the send-time read of `lowerings` and the serve's read of trust/hold/lowerings one step under K's record lock (C39.4 server level, no RPC wait under it), like callbacks' tick. The raise's step applies any due end, checks conditions 3-7 and writes trust; 5.6, point 6 states it. callbacks.md 5.6, point 11 gains the same "K step" paragraph and its record row says `lowerings` changes only in a K step. Test KA21 (interleaving search). A CAS on `lowerings` alone was rejected because the raise also depends on the lapse check and the fence values, which a single-word CAS does not cover.

#### GLM 2 (minor)

Location: docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:1126-1127 (10.5, KA8, first clause; with 5.2 `ka.deadline` at 493-494 and 5.6, point 6, condition 7)

Defect (verbatim):
> The acceptance case "A reply received 121 seconds after its send (the simulator holds it). No raise." cannot exercise condition 7's "the receipt is within E of the send": with `ka.deadline` = 30 s and E = 120 s, every keepalive call is ended by DeadlineExceeded at 30 s, so a datagram arriving at 121 s matches no live call and fails condition 1 (not `Completed`), never condition 7. The E-at-receipt guard is unreachable with these caps, and the test passes for the wrong reason — an implementation with no E-at-receipt check at all still passes KA8. Fix: cover the receipt-age bound by delaying only the handling of the outcome (as the second clause already does), or rewrite the first clause as an explicit DeadlineExceeded/dropped-datagram case.

Consequence (verbatim):
> The test suite appears to cover the evidence-age bound but proves nothing about it; a regression that deletes the E-at-receipt condition would go undetected by KA8 (defense-in-depth check silently untested).

Disposition: FIXED. KA8 rewritten: the receipt-within-E guard is tested at the seam with a scripted Rpc returning a Reply received E+1 s after its send; the Rx path is stated as DeadlineExceeded (a failed keepalive); the handling-delay case kept.

#### DeepSeek 1 (major)

Location: docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:672

Defect (verbatim):
> The schedule is defined as 'every due time ... counted from the previous keepalive's send stamp' (also 5.6.4, 5.6.8, section 6.1), and `ka_in_flight` stores 'its send stamp once known' (line 577). But a failed keepalive's send stamp is never known: the seams `Rpc::call` returns `Reply.first_sent` only on `Completed`; `RpcError::Aborted` carries `ReplyEvidence` (family, local_generation, epoch, conn_id, call_number, received) and `NotSent`/`NoReply` carry no stamp (seams-and-scheduling.md 5.4 point 2). Q5 (line 1336) only notes housekeeping has no `from` 'for its first keepalive'; it does not cover any later failed keepalive. Fix: require #71 to return the hand-off/first-sent stamp on every outcome (or have the keepalive owner record a hand-off stamp), and state that `ka.min_gap` and the failure gap are measured from that stamp, with a defined conservative fallback when no sample exists.

Consequence (verbatim):
> A server that stops answering produces `Timeout`/`NoReply`; the client must then schedule the next keepalive at min_gap/doubled gap but has no send stamp from which to build the boot-clock `Deadline`. Implemented as written, it must either use a `from` outside the sampling function's allowed sources (violating #71 5.3 point 2 and the xtask check) or use the scheduling tick, which is earlier than the actual hand-off, so the next send can occur sooner than `ka.min_gap` after the previous actual send, weakening the rate/identity bound exactly on the failure path.

Disposition: FIXED, with the proposed fix adapted. A self-recorded hand-off stamp cannot be passed as `from`: #71 5.3, point 2's call-site check accepts only a request's `.arrival` or a Reply's `first_sent`, so a fresh sample the keepalive owner takes fails that check as written. New field `ka_from` (5.6, point 1): after Completed it is the reply's `first_sent`; after any other outcome it is the arrival the housekeeping step that handled the outcome records. That step runs after the call ended, so the gap from it is never shorter than from the true send (rate and identity bounds hold). It needs no new demand beyond Q5's existing item (housekeeping steps record an arrival); 5.6, point 5 says the failure path's code waits for it. Section 6, point 1 uses the same rule on a failed build.

#### DeepSeek 2 (major)

Location: docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:767

Defect (verbatim):
> The failed-keepalive rule says 'Every outcome other than `Completed` ... is a failed keepalive' and lowers trust/raises `ka_failures`, but the enumerated set omits outcomes the same spec handles differently: `StartRefused` (5.6.4, line 625: open a new connection, nothing sent), `IdentityExhausted` (5.6.4, line 633: no keepalive, identity_skip), `DeadlineInvalid` (section 6.1), and the seams' client-local `NotSent(Admission/Cancelled/Closed/ClockFailed/Unreachable)`. callbacks.md 5.6.11's no-valid-reply list (Timeout, DeadlineExceeded, Aborted, KeyExhausted) does not include a call that was never sent. Fix: define the failed-keepalive set as outcomes of a started call that was handed to the transport (or an abort), and give local non-send outcomes their own rule (do not lower trust and do not bump `ka_failures`; retry/open a connection as appropriate).

Consequence (verbatim):
> A local refusal such as `NotSent(Admission)` (possible when local fetch traffic fills the in-flight budget) or a `StartRefused` retry is counted as a server loss: trust is lowered and the keepalive gap doubles, so local congestion or an off-path flood that induces admission pressure can keep a healthy server in bounded revalidation and delay recovery, misattributing a client-local failure to the server. If the broad sentence is not applied to these outcomes, their handling is simply undefined.

Disposition: FIXED. 5.6, point 8 now splits outcomes into a failed keepalive (handed to the transport, no valid reply: malformed Completed, Aborted, Timeout, DeadlineExceeded/KeyExhausted with sent_any, ReplyTooLarge; #71's Aborted and NoReply Deadline/PeerLost/ReplyTooLarge/Security) and a local outcome (StartRefused, IdentityExhausted, DeadlineInvalid, ProtocolViolation, Deadline/KeyExhausted without sent_any, Fenced, Cancelled; every NotSent; NoReply Cancelled/Closed/NetworkChanged/ClockFailed), which neither lowers trust nor raises ka_failures and retries after ka.min_gap. KA22 added; counters by kind.

#### DeepSeek 3 (major)

Location: docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:855

Defect (verbatim):
> 5.7 point 5 states the server's time 'never feeds a deadline ... no ... expiry the client counts is computed from it', and the section 8 row (line 1017) repeats 'the time feeds no deadline'. But 5.7 point 6 (lines 878-880) shortens a promise's lifetime by D derived from the keepalive reply's server time when the server's clock is ahead, and a promise expiry is a boot-clock deadline (C37.1). Fix: qualify point 5 and the section 8 row to 'no deadline except the shortening of point 6' (or restate the shortening as the only permitted use), so the two normative points agree.

Consequence (verbatim):
> The central security bound is self-contradictory: an implementer following point 5's 'no expiry the client counts is computed from it' omits the D shortening, so a promise with an absolute server-clock expiry on a server whose clock is ahead is relied on up to `cb.promise.lifetime_max` although point 6 requires shortening it; an implementer following point 6 contradicts point 5. The spec cannot be implemented or reviewed unambiguously as written.

Disposition: FIXED. 5.7, point 5 now says server time never sets or lengthens a deadline and its one use on an expiry is point 6's shortening; the section 8 row says the same.

#### DeepSeek 4 (minor)

Location: docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:655

Defect (verbatim):
> 5.6 point 5's early causes are only: K becoming active, a network-generation change, an episode, and a lowering. 3.2 (line 317) promises an early keepalive after 'a resume', and 5.6 point 6's failure handling (line 718) names 'an advance' as a cause that should make one due 'as point 5 says'. A socket pause/resume raises the pause generation, and a reset advances the suspect epoch, but neither is in point 5's list (nor is an epoch advance a lowering). Fix: add an advance and a pause/resume (every fence value whose change can fail condition 4) to 5.6 point 5, or remove them from 3.2 and 5.6 point 6.

Consequence (verbatim):
> After a reset (rows R1/B0) or an Rx pause/resume, a keepalive in flight fails condition 4, but no early keepalive is scheduled; the next one waits the steady-state gap of up to 60 s. The failure-handling sentence is then unimplementable as written, and trust restoration is delayed (entries are suspect, so this is availability, not staleness).

Disposition: FIXED. Early causes (5.6, point 5) now include an advance of K's suspect epoch and a raise of the pause generation of K's family's socket (every change that can fail condition 4, 5 or 6); 3.2 and point 6's stale-reply list match.

#### DeepSeek 7 (minor)

Location: docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:788

Defect (verbatim):
> 5.6 point 9's A14 bound assumes 'the keepalive is such a call, made at least every ka.interval while K is active'. 5.6 point 4 (line 633) explicitly stops sending when `ka.identity_share` is exhausted, and section 6.1 (line 949) stops sending when the clock sample or deadline build fails; in both cases the gap can exceed `ka.interval`. Fix: qualify the premise with those cases and state the bound as min(ka.interval + ka.deadline, E after the last receipt).

Consequence (verbatim):
> The claimed A14 bound (a dropped promise served under trust for at most `ka.interval` + `ka.deadline` = 90 s) can be exceeded by such a suppressed keepalive; trust actually lasts until E (120 s) after the last reply. The safe C32 bound still holds, but the stated quantitative guarantee is false.

Disposition: FIXED. 5.6, point 9 restated: served under trust until the earlier of the next keepalive's outcome and the last raise's end (E after receipt); on schedule at most ka.interval + ka.deadline, a suppressed keepalive at most E; so at most max(90 s, 120 s) = 120 s.

#### DeepSeek 8 (minor)

Location: docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:583

Defect (verbatim):
> 5.6 point 1 claims every keepalive field is fixed-size and lists only per-K fields, but 5.6 point 4's enforcement of `ka.identity_share` requires counting 'live keepalive connections plus their reserved retired identities' for R = 1980 s. The retired-pair deadlines for up to 2048 pairs are state that is neither in the record table nor in a named global table, and rx.md/the seams expose no count query. Fix: specify where the retired-pair tracker lives (a fixed 2048-entry table or per-K bounded array with R eviction) and include it in the state bound.

Consequence (verbatim):
> The claimed identity bound is not shown to be enforceable: an implementation either cannot count retired pairs (and can exceed the share, starving data-call identities) or must keep an unbounded list, contradicting the 'fixed-size fields' claim. The answer to 'is all keepalive state bounded' is therefore unproven.

Disposition: FIXED. 5.6, point 1 adds one client-wide table of ka.identity_share fixed slots (free, live, or reserved until close + R); opening takes a free slot; 5.6, point 4 and the derived bounds use it.

#### DeepSeek 9 (minor)

Location: docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:636

Defect (verbatim):
> The keepalive connection is kept between keepalives (up to 60 s), but neither 5.6 point 4 nor 5.2 sets the connection's user-set idle retirement time, and rx.md 5.6 point 2 retires an outgoing connection after 'the time its user set when opening it'. The only reconnect cases named are the four-channel exhaustion and StartRefused. Fix: set `ka_conn`'s idle retirement to at least `ka.interval` + `ka.deadline` (or keep it alive), and define the connection-retired/Fenced outcome as a local reconnect, not a failed keepalive.

Consequence (verbatim):
> If the connection's idle time is less than the inter-keepalive gap, Rx retires it silently; the next start ends `NotSent(Closed)`/`Fenced` (or StartRefused), which 5.6 point 8's broad rule treats as a failed keepalive, lowering trust and doubling the backoff for a local retirement. The steady-state 11-pair identity bound also assumes the connection survives four gaps, so it would be wrong under early retirement.

Disposition: FIXED. New cap ka.conn_idle = 120 s (2 × ka.interval), the idle time the keepalive connection is opened with; an Rx retirement or fence is a local outcome (5.6, point 8) and the connection is reopened.

#### DeepSeek 10 (minor)

Location: docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:800

Defect (verbatim):
> 5.6 point 10 asserts 'no order lets a raise survive a lowering that was recorded before it', but the check-and-write is not atomic: the core can increment `lowerings` and write `trust` = none after the housekeeping thread has read `lowerings` (condition 6) and before it writes the raised trust. The raise then overwrites trust = none while `lowerings` has risen. Fix: make the raise's trust write conditional on the checked `lowerings` value (compare-and-swap) or re-read and re-check after writing.

Consequence (verbatim):
> Trust can survive a failed-call lowering recorded between the check and the write; entries validated after that lowering are then served under trust from a keepalive sent before the lowering, contrary to condition 6's purpose. The entry-count comparison still keeps entries validated before the lowering out of the trusted branch, so this is an ordering-invariant defect rather than a stale-data path, but the stated guarantee is not provided by the described state.

Disposition: FIXED (same as GLM major; minor here). See the GLM major's disposition.

#### Round 2, item 6

Finding (verbatim):
> minor docs/specs/keepalive-and-server-time.md:596-601, 647-655 and 841 | NEW. Disposition: FIXED. A failed open (IdentityExhausted) frees its slot at once (#81 5.6, point 1). Uncertain items the seats raised in round 2, with the notes made on them:

- The K step's lock order against volume and object locks, and the connection layer's writes of `hold`: not changed. A K step takes only the server key's record lock and is never taken under a volume or object lock (a serve reads trust, hold and the lowering count first, server level first, ADR 0004 C39, point 4). Writes of `hold` are not K steps; a stale read delays the hold's start by one serve, and the hold's exit advance (callbacks.md 5.6, point 12) fences what was recorded before it.
- A gap counted from `first_sent` can be shorter than the true gap by the hand-off margin: accepted as stated; the margin lies inside `ka.deadline` and shortens one gap only.
- A clock sample inside the K step: ADR 0004 C39, point 4, forbids RPC waits, disk writes and kernel notifications under a lock; a clock read is none of them. Not changed.
