# Review record: the FUSE codec, part 1

## The change

Class S. Issue #92 (plan id M2a-29), part 1.

- The specification `docs/specs/fuse-codec.md` (protocol, class S, draft)
  and register rows E70 to E74: the kernel's FUSE uapi header at v7.3 and
  v3.15, its FUSE documentation, fuse(4), and the uapi limits, stat and
  errno headers. No FUSE library's source and no part of the kernel's
  FUSE implementation was read.
- A new layer crate, `crates/ironafs-fuse`, with no third-party
  dependency:
  - INIT negotiation from 7.23 to 7.46;
  - the reviewed allowlist and never-list, and the selection function;
  - decoding of the requests a read-only mount needs, STATX included;
  - replies reachable only through a session's single-use, shape-typed
    tokens and its `finish`;
  - the Linux error numbers for the contract's errors.
- Not in this part, and still #92's: READDIR's reply records, the
  invalidation notifications, the extended-attribute, sync and seek
  requests, the requests that change a volume, the reserved operations,
  and the fuzz target (issue #45).
- **Written by** `claude-code:claude-opus-5-5` (a Claude Code subagent,
  test first).

## Findings

Round 1 (each quoted verbatim, with its verification and fix, in the
controller's fix log):

- **Major (GPT): STATX on the not-supported path.** Fixed (`8b7071a`,
  `43ffbea`) by serving STATX. ADR 0004 L2, point 4, forbids `ENOSYS` for
  an operation a volume could later support, and STATX's birth time is one
  (N47, point 6). Lowering the top minor would give up
  `FUSE_NO_EXPORT_SUPPORT`.
- **Major (GPT): INIT decoding.** Fixed: the major is read first; a
  major-7 body is exactly 16 bytes, or 64 with `FUSE_INIT_EXT`; each
  allowlisted flag is accepted only from its minor.
- **Major (GPT): replies could bypass the encoders.** Fixed: typed,
  single-use reply tokens and an opaque `Reply`.
- **Minors (GPT):** OPEN's second word from 7.33; `map_alignment` dated
  7.31; BATCH_FORGET read as fixed records. All fixed.
- **Minor (GLM): the spec's allocation claim.** Fixed: a LOOKUP's name is
  the one copy, after its 255-byte cap.

Round 2:

- **Major (GPT): tokens could be minted twice** by decoding one frame
  twice. Fixed (`cd3d6f7`, `7488d31`) with `Session`, the only public
  decoder: one token per pending id, and `finish` as the only way to a
  reply's bytes.
- **Major (GPT): the INIT token took another INIT's result.** Fixed:
  `InitReply::answer` negotiates with its own offer and encodes the one
  reply its major allows.
- **Minors (GPT):** STATX below 7.39 is refused with `EOPNOTSUPP`; §8 no
  longer says nothing is allocated from a decoded length. Both fixed.

Round 3, each item new with round 2's session unless marked:

- **Major: two INITs could both hold tokens**, and the second finished
  could replace the first's minor. Fixed (`ba01367`): one INIT token at a
  time; a second INIT is refused with an error reply that settles
  nothing.
- **Major: `Reply`'s derived `Debug` printed its bytes.** Fixed: a manual
  `Debug` shows the id and length only.
- **Major: `pending` had no limit;** a dropped token or reply leaves its
  id recorded; a wrong-session `finish` consumed the reply. Fixed:
  - a cap of 4,096 pending ids, past which a request is refused with no
    reply and the layer ends the connection;
  - a dropped token or reply stays recorded until the session ends,
    bounded by the cap, as the spec states;
  - `finish` hands a refused reply back unwritten.
- **Minor: the duplicate check exempted no-reply opcodes.** Fixed: a
  request of any opcode whose id is pending is refused.
- **Minor: the arbitrary-bytes property never finished INIT.** Fixed: its
  negotiated branch answers and finishes an INIT through the public API,
  then decodes the bytes once.
- **Major, partly fixed (round 2's first item): `decode` still mints a
  token for any well-formed frame**, including after a replay of a
  retired id. Not redesigned, by the controller's direction. A codec
  cannot tell the kernel's bytes from a caller's, and the kernel reuses an
  answered id, so a replay after `finish` is the protocol. The spec states
  the boundary (5.6, point 3): authority over what reaches the session is
  the device descriptor's, held by the device reader (M2a-30). The pull
  request lists it as an open item.

Round 4 (scoped), all fixed in `f58a12c`:

- **Minor: the INIT guard tracked only decoded INITs.** A malformed first
  INIT, owed its error reply, did not stop a valid second INIT from
  negotiating. Fixed in the code, not by narrowing the spec. The guard now
  holds while any INIT's token is outstanding before the protocol is
  settled, a refused INIT's error token included. Test seen failing first:
  `session::tests::a_malformed_init_owed_a_reply_holds_the_init_guard`.
- **Minor: the boundary sentence was too broad.** It said every
  well-formed frame with an unrecorded id gets a token. It now names the
  exceptions: requests that take no reply, and requests past the cap.
- **Minor: section 7 omitted `Overloaded`** and left `InitPending`'s `EIO`
  ambiguous beside `EPROTO`. Section 7 now lists the three refusals sent
  with no reply (an opcode that takes none, `DuplicateUnique`,
  `Overloaded`). It separates INIT refused by negotiation (`EPROTO`) from
  INIT refused by the decoder, `InitPending` included (`EIO`).
