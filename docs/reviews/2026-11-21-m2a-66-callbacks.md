# Review record: the callback service specification

## The change

Class S. Issue #66 (plan id M2a-04). This is the first version, written
from documents only; the issue stays open for the capture-backed revision
and the test-cell revision (callbacks.md, section 13, Q1 and Q2).

- `docs/specs/callbacks.md`: the callback service's registration with Rx;
  the closed allowlist of callback procedures, with the role, source and
  status of each; the unsupported-operation abort and what is discarded
  without a reply; the fields read, their caps and hostile-input rules;
  the replies; the callback state record per server key and the fence
  tuple the cache records and compares; ADR 0004 C31's table restated
  against the specification; the advance tick that orders Rx's overload
  confirmations and over-limit work after the suspect epoch moves; ADR
  0006's callback points (an ABORT or BUSY on an incoming call, an ABORT
  as "an error", the hold and its exit); what the sources say on
  assumption A14; counters, events and tests.
- `docs/specs/README.md`: the index row, and register rows E11 (the AFS
  registry's callback procedure table, client capability list, service
  list and error tables), E12 (the capabilities query draft) and E13 (the
  callback extensions draft). Numbered E11 because the VL and FS read
  specification (#65) uses E5 to E10 on its branch; E11 and E12 overlap
  that specification's rows for the same pages and text, and are merged
  with them once both are on `main`.
- **Written by** `claude-code:claude-opus-5-5` (drafting, and the fixes of
  every round).
- **Merge order.** The specification cites ADR 0006 and the Rx and VL and
  FS read specifications; it merges after the pull requests that land
  them.

## Findings

### Round 1 (DeepSeek)

- **Blocker, fixed:** the fence recorded and compared only the server
  key's values, while ADR 0004 C16, point 2, needs the object's, the
  volume's and the server's break counters recorded at send and compared
  before a reply is used. The fence tuple is now the object's and
  volume's break counters, the suspect epoch, the socket's pause
  generation and a resume generation, recorded per object and compared
  at install and on every serve (5.6, points 2 to 4; C2 now breaks the
  fetched file, and its volume, while the fetch is in flight).
- **Major, fixed:** C31's row for a break naming no files had no row for
  an empty CallBack. New rows B0 (known server: the server advances at
  the tick) and B5 (unmatched source: counted); test C21.
- **Major, fixed:** extended callbacks were refused unread, against ADR
  0004 N22, point 7 (an unprotected one is acted on as a plain break).
  ExtendedCallBack (65543) is now listed: its extension part is never
  used, and until its layout is captured it is acted on as the widest
  plain break its source allows, a server-wide advance (rows X1, X2). The
  draft's number 65540 stays refused, because the registry assigns it to
  a diagnostic query (README rule 7), with a capture trigger in section
  11.
- **Major, fixed:** C32, point 5's clock comparison on every serve was
  quoted but not carried out. A resume generation, raised when the C37
  clocks show a suspend or jump, is part of every fence tuple (test C22).
- **Major, fixed by a different route:** an identity call over the rate
  limit advanced the server's epoch, against C31's probe row. The
  reviewer proposed discarding over-rate probes with counters only; that
  would drop work over the rate limit without an advance, which C31's
  rate-limit row forbids. Instead identity calls are no longer charged
  to any bucket and never wait: each is answered from a prebuilt reply
  in constant time, so none is ever more than the service can process,
  and the service moves no epoch for a probe (5.6, point 8).
- **Major, fixed:** a retired callback state record's suspect epoch
  restarted at 0, so an old entry could match a new record. Epochs now
  come from one client-wide counter that never repeats a value (test
  C23).
- **Minors, fixed:** an over-cap identity call had no row (row I4); the
  file-id array cap's reason did not reproduce its value; the rate-limit
  cap's scope and the work unit were unclear; a break flood could starve
  identity calls (a fairness rule, test C24); ProbeUuid's behaviour while
  pending was undefined (refused with −455 until settled).
- **Uncertain items:** eleven; six were resolved by the fixes above, five
  recorded with no change (whether servers send empty breaks; the
  registry and draft facts being UNVERIFIED by the specification's own
  labels; cross-specification references; the flood bound being a
  judgement; the hold's exit outside the tick, a fixture-only path).

### Round 2 (DeepSeek verification; GLM full review)

- **Major (DeepSeek), fixed:** a probe flood that fills Rx's caps still
  produces overload events that advance epochs, so "not possible" was
  false. The specification now states the true bound: the service moves
  no epoch for a probe; a flood that reaches Rx's caps is an overload
  like any other, advancing at most once per `cb.advance_interval` and
  only if a call recorded the current epoch (C31's rate-limit row; Rx
  cannot tell a held probe from a held break). C11 tests both sides of
  the cap. Reserving Rx admission for identity calls was not taken: Rx
  knows nothing of AFS procedures (ADR 0004 C4, point 4).
- **Major (DeepSeek), fixed:** the resume generation was raised only on
  serve, so a reply in flight across a suspend could be installed. One
  edge-triggered fence check now runs at install and at serve (5.6,
  point 2), keepalive installs included; C22 tests the in-flight case
  with no serve between.
- **Major (DeepSeek), fixed:** the client-wide epoch counter had no
  synchronisation rule. It is one atomic fetch-and-add with sequentially
  consistent ordering, the only way any thread takes a value; its
  exhaustion is stated.
- **Minors (DeepSeek), fixed:** row I4 was missing from the discard list;
  refusals and probes shared one queue (now three queues, test C24
  extended); the identity route's cost is stated as a residual (no
  amplification above twice the bytes received for a peer Rx has not
  verified; a verified peer bounded by Rx's caps and the callback
  thread).
- **Minors (GLM), fixed:** the work-unit charge was stated three ways
  (now one: 1 plus file ids times cells); point 8's "never delayed"
  contradicted the queueing; the golden vectors omitted 65543. GLM's
  suggested bytes for 65543 (`0000ffc7`) were refuted: 65543 is
  2¹⁶ + 7, so the vector is `00010007`.
- **Uncertain items:** DeepSeek nine, GLM eight. Fixed from them: the
  fence check is edge-triggered; a reply showing the server reset the
  client's callback state lowers trust (5.6, point 11); the tick runs
  as one uninterrupted step; C11 states its premise. The rest were
  resolved by the fixes or recorded with no change.

### Round 3 (verification)

Both PASS. All minors fixed in `27678e6`:

- **Minor (DeepSeek and GLM):** the burst's reason said a largest break
  in three cells passes the 8 192-unit burst; three cells is 8 191 units,
  so it is four or more.
- **Minor (DeepSeek and GLM):** point 8 said identity calls are "never
  dropped", while rows I2 and I4 discard some; it now says "never dropped
  for load".
- **Minor (DeepSeek):** an exhausted epoch counter failed a call with an
  undefined "generic failure". The specification now defines its one
  local outcome, `EpochExhausted`: nothing is sent, and the operation
  fails with ADR 0004 C46, point 1's "unreachable" (section 7, point 6).

Residuals the reviewers accepted as stated: a probe flood past Rx's caps
can advance a server once per interval; a verified peer can draw identity
replies at its own rate; refusals starve behind breaks and probes until
Rx's reply deadline ends them; forged breaks naming one file can make
its fetches fail their fence (section 13, Q10).

## Decision 29 amendment

After the three rounds, the cache's fence code (#91, its second part)
found that the specifications disagreed on what the fence compares. The
controller decided, as its decision 29 of 2026-11-21: the cache's fence
is this specification's five values plus the context's local volume
epoch; the credential and rights generations are the ticket's only; the
seams specification's overload record is replaced; the lock design is
kept, with the fence table's lock a leaf in C39, point 4's order and
held on the serve path, as the API enforces; and compare-and-swap
wording replaces "fetch-and-add". This specification owns the tuple, so
it is amended first; the incarnation (#78), seams (#71) and contract
(#67) specifications follow in their own changes. The amendment
describes what #91's implementation does.

- Section 1: quotes C16, point 3, C25, point 3, and C39, point 4.
- Sections 3 and 4: the tuple has six values; new terms for the local
  volume epoch, the fence table and the ticket.
- 5.6, point 1: the suspect epoch counter is a compare-and-swap that
  never wraps; the field is written and read under the fence table's
  lock.
- 5.6, point 2: the six values and where each comes from; what is not
  in the tuple (the ticket's generations; no overload counter; the push
  to a binding in its own process is #67's); values that never repeat,
  eviction and exhaustion; the order of the check's steps; the fence
  table's lock.
- 5.6, point 3: the local volume epoch's write path, and the adoption
  rule for a call sent while its context held no copy.
- Section 6, point 3: compare-and-swap wording.
- Section 9: rows for C16, point 3, C25, point 3, and C39, point 4.
- 10.5: acceptance case C25, the local volume epoch.

Reviewed on its own diff (`b85db41..63313e5`): GLM PASS with two
minors; DeepSeek FAIL with one major and one minor. The author of the
incarnation amendment (#78), applying this text, found two more.
